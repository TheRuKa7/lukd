## Editorial assessment and limits

The opening now promises a person seeking ordinary belonging inside an institution that misclassifies them. Its hook joins the personal goal to an immediate unjust seizure. Humour comes from paid work, literal interpretations and interrupted competence; the dangerous scenes retain time for fear, relief and disagreement. Nil's chosen name is a social decision, not a reward from a machine. Vesper and Maeve each make a consequential choice before becoming companions.

The draft is approximately13,700 body words, below the rough15,000–21,000 per-part planning guideline. Chapter5 is also slightly below the approximate1,500-word chapter guideline. These are recorded length deviations, not hidden completion claims. No additional scene was inserted solely to satisfy a count. Phase6 must reassess pace and the125,000-word whole-novel target using all seven completed parts, with reader feedback where available. The remaining42 chapters are not written.

The Varro sequence deliberately spells out the rules for its first use. Its explanatory density needs particular attention in Phase6 and the later panel adaptation. This prose is not a frame-by-frame timing or damage simulation. It establishes choices, legal pattern order and visible consequences; it does not prove runtime balance or154 pages of manga pacing.

No native manga pages, new image generations, paid services, game builds or publication were performed in Phase4. Seven substantive6,000-line contexts remain Phase7 work; current short research dossiers are still recorded as incomplete. Independent fresh-reader acceptance has not been obtained.
