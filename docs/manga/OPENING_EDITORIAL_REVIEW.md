## The opening's dramatic promise

A person who cannot be counted wants a place in ordinary life. They risk that possibility to protect someone the system can count but will not protect. Naming gives them a chosen resonance, but fails to make them registered; their attempt to belong instead makes them hunted. An apparent mercenary stays, while a feared collector is shown to have someone he cannot leave. By the end of chapter03, NIL has chosen to spare a person without knowing whether it is mathematically advantageous.

The reader should want to know who NIL is because they care what NIL does. NaN is a visible obstacle, not the only personality trait. The mysterious origin answer belongs at the series midpoint; the opening must already satisfy its own smaller emotional question.

Recommended chapter titles, matching the integrating treatment:

1. **No Account**: an attempted ordinary transaction becomes rescue, refuge and an alarming Naming.
2. **Seven Fen**: survival makes cooperation useful, then a voluntary action makes trust possible.
3. **A Receipt for Mercy**: an enemy's debt exposes a human motive; NIL makes a costly choice and takes a causal route out of Meridian.

## Information release and mystery hygiene

By chapter03 the reader should understand only what the opening has demonstrated: an allocation affects access; NIL's identity fails to register; a chosen resonance is different from that identity; power changes a declared possibility using a source; people can survive an exhausted Audit stake; the hunt comes from the Naming alert; and a toll record supplies the next lead.

The reader may suspect connections between NIL, old ledgers and the institution. Do not establish a three-century personal lifespan, a specific parentage, the complete Remainder account, Hap's confinement or the final translation equation. Those facts require later evidence. The midpoint reveal must change how NIL acts, not merely confirm a glossary entry already delivered in chapter01.

Vesper's guilt should first leak through avoidance and overprotective decisions. Varro's exemption must look plausible to him until the relevant authorization/release evidence is found. Thale's care is genuine but selective; later exposure of its hidden debit should revise the reader's interpretation of earlier competent actions.

## Page-turn and dialogue rules

- A page turns because a choice, threat, discovery or relationship changes, not because a wide pose needs another picture.
- Put a question and its emotionally important answer on different beats. Leave the answer's reaction visible.
- After an impact, give the reader a body, damaged object or changed position that proves its effect.
- Use a close-up when the thought changes. A sequence of similar handsome faces does not create an emotional arc.
- Banter is an exchange of intentions. NIL can answer literally; Vesper can change the terms; either can be unexpectedly kind. Three unrelated quips are not chemistry.
- The calm after danger needs at least one unhurried beat. A joke may release tension once the person in danger is safe; grief is not the punchline.
- Introduce no more named institutions than the current decision requires. An explanation that can be postponed without hurting comprehension should be postponed.
- Keep English left-to-right/top-to-bottom reading order explicit in the production brief and every layout review. Do not alternate conventions to mimic a reference.
- Tiny repeated score captions are not a substitute for a visible cause and outcome. Exact readouts must remain internally correct when shown.

## Failure patterns to reject in Mangaka review

1. **Chronology without consequence:** rescue, shrine, fight and market appear in sequence but none causes the next. Fix the escape route, alarm, blocked exits and specific lead.
2. **Stoic slideshow:** faces remain identical across fear, hope and relief. Rewrite the acting instruction and use the actual emotional turn as the panel's subject.
3. **Generic kindness:** NIL saves a stranger without risking their own want. Show that becoming unregistered trouble can destroy the ordinary belonging they seek.
4. **Mechanics lecture:** the reader receives seven classifiers before seeing one useful decision. Demonstrate the smallest necessary rule in action; reserve depth for later choices.
5. **Motive as absolution:** Varro's sister erases what he did. Let motive make mercy difficult and meaningful while keeping his responsibility intact.
6. **Premature answer:** a dream or narrator explains the hidden account or cage early. Replace the answer with a specific observation whose meaning changes later.
7. **False scale:** every small hit gets a splash and every page reaches the same volume. Save the largest image for a changed situation; use silence and close framing elsewhere.
8. **Decorative geography:** a pretty city backdrop changes between panels without a traveled route. Require an establishing position and a motivated cut before a location switch.
9. **Assembly-line chapter endings:** every chapter adds a bigger enemy. Chapter01 changes status, chapter02 changes relationship under threat, chapter03 changes the protagonist's chosen conduct and destination.

## Editorial acceptance questions

Before generating later chapters, review chapter01 in reading order and answer these from the actual pages, not from the outline:

- What does NIL want before the rescue, and which action risks it?
- Can the reader identify where Ama's six restored units came from?
- Is there a recognizable difference between NIL's hope and disappointment at Naming?
- Does the alarm follow a voluntary choice with understandable intent?
- Is the last-page question stronger than "who is this impressive-looking person"?
- Can the key scene be understood from poses and reactions when most captions are covered?
- Does at least one ordinary human act make Meridian feel inhabited?
- Are mysteries withheld while the immediate event remains clear?

These are editorial gates. Passing them in a planning document is not evidence of finished Mangaka pages, player comprehension, external cultural review or reader enjoyment.
