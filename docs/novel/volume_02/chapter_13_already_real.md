# Volume Two: The Unfinished Hundred

## Chapter 13: Already Real

Before the Audit, Hina asked to see the hairpin again.

Tamamo took her to the side room. Nil waited outside the screen until Hina beckoned them in.

The woman beside the hairpin was awake. She had a little book open on her knees, although the hand beneath the page was too faint to turn it. An attendant waited without reaching in.

“Would you turn it?” the woman asked Hina.

Hina did. The next page contained an untidy drawing of a house with a room added at the back.

“My keeper thought I would like a window,” the woman said. “I said I wanted a cupboard for my things first. She drew both.”

Hina studied the pencilled cupboard.

“Was she a good drawer?”

“Terrible. It fell down in the first plan.”

She smiled. Her face thinned around the smile, then returned. Hina looked quickly at the hairpin so the woman would not have to watch her noticing.

“Do you want the permanence?” she asked.

“I'd like to finish this book.”

“And after?”

“I would like there to be an after in which I can decide.”

Hina nodded. It was a small nod, without any of her practised grandeur.

Outside the screen, Tamamo stood beside the care roster.

“She was less stable last week,” she said. “The existing renewals are holding, but they don't hold everyone equally. I will not use her wish as a vote she hasn't given. I am telling you why I made the offer.”

“I wanted to say yes,” Hina said.

Nil's throat tightened.

Hina looked at them, making herself finish. “Not to sending you away. To the part where I wouldn't wake up wondering whether my hands would be there.”

Nil had no reassurance big enough for that. They offered the only thing they knew.

“I heard you.”

Tamamo laid a hand on the roster. “Then do not mistake refusing me for making that fear disappear.”

“I won't,” Hina said.

***

The Audit room had no throne and nine windows.

Each window held a small reserve vessel behind its lattice. Fine channels joined the vessels to the luminous tips of Tamamo's tails, then returned through a central control frame. Nil could see where the court's private challenge allotment entered the apparatus. Ao showed them the physical separation from the care lines in the next wing.

“These are the only reserves offered,” he said. “The household locks do not open into this room.”

Maeve traced the isolation joint before accepting the witness copy. Aka tested the release bar. An attendant lifted each window's emergency hood so that the actual route could still be seen if the images filled the room.

The practice strikers were calibrated at the same fifty-unit base as Enma's demonstration. The court funded both bounded sides from its declared challenge allotment; closing the trial would settle control of that allotment and nothing else. No one would receive money or a new Civic allocation. The promised negotiation concerned the office key, not Tamamo's body or the people under her care.

Vesper checked the actual-stop windows, then the return paths. “And if your images hide one of those?”

“Call the condition,” Tamamo said. “We pause while the witness checks it.”

“Put that in.”

Ao added the sentence. Nil was beginning to understand that Vesper's refusal to be impressed by good intentions was work, too.

Hina laid her household witness tag beside the others. Her local 9 channel could mark a nine as an authorized party face rather than an unclaimed charge under the old court convention. She showed Nil the difference before fastening it. The authorization was a small, named use of her own standing route, not a gift of years or a promise to absorb any loss they chose.

“You can withdraw it,” Nil said.

“Yes.” She looked at the emergency hood. “I want to stay.”

Maeve took the observer's station beside Aka. Vesper kept her revolver in the sealed rack. Her hands would stop wheels; a bullet would contribute nothing to the agreed claim.

Tamamo stepped into the centre of the room.

For an instant all nine tail digits brightened. Then the windows multiplied.

Nil saw eighteen, then too many to count. The room lengthened behind Tamamo; a second floor shone beneath the first. The painted rails seemed to turn around the pillars, offering paths that led back into themselves.

“The marked platform remains where it was,” Ao said from somewhere behind the images.

Nil lowered their eyes. Three brass studs lay at the edge of the actual platform. They had counted them before the room changed. They counted them again.

The first wheel began.

A 7 came toward their palm. Nil stopped it.

The actual window read 8.

Their stomach dropped. They had known the image could lie. Knowledge had not made the lie look any less like what they wanted.

Vesper stopped 3. Carry advanced its scoring face to 4. Nil caught 0 on the last position.

Eight, four, zero. No pattern.

A warning shook through the floor. The return shutters began to close. Nil moved toward the post they remembered, only to see it vanish and reappear farther away.

“Down, by me,” Vesper said.

Nil went down. Hina lowered the umbrella to shield their view from the brightest image, not their bodies from an impossible blow. Behind its paper the real brass studs stood out. Nil reached the return catch and found zero by the actual marker.

The closing load struck the frame instead of driving through their declared stake. A safety rail rattled under Vesper's hand. Nothing had hit Tamamo, and one of the court's tail gauges had nevertheless lost part of its allotted capacity in the exchange.

Hina was trembling. The umbrella's tongue had withdrawn completely.

“You said these were pictures,” Nil whispered.

“They are,” she whispered back. “That doesn't mean they're easy.”

Tamamo watched them through the moving light. She did not look pleased at the fear. She did not stop using it.

“The court's protections are an image too?” she asked. “Something you can refuse because you have found a more attractive shape?”

Hina's jaw tightened. Nil felt the urge to answer for her and held it back.

“We saw the care room,” Hina said. “I'm not refusing the people in it.”

She moved the umbrella until its eye could look beneath the projecting lattice. The old patched corner caught a sliver of light that the newer paper transmitted differently.

“There,” she said.

Nil followed her gaze. The false face moved through the bright part. Beneath it, close to the rim, the actual engraving caught the low light from the emergency hood.

The real 5 looked less beautiful than the false 7. Nil waited until it was truly at the stop.

Five.

The striker registered 55. Carry lifted once.

Vesper took 6. In the scoring frame it became 7. The actual 6 stayed beside it where nobody could make it disappear.

Nil took 7.

Five, seven, seven. A Pair.

The channel closed through two of the tail reserves and lowered a third. The images on that side of the room went out. Behind them was an ordinary wall with a repaired crack above the skirting.

Maeve breathed out at the observer's station. Aka was writing the actual and scored faces in separate columns.

“Three routes partially exposed,” he said.

“Two closed,” Ao corrected. “One remains live.”

Aka changed the entry. Neither hid the correction.

Tamamo's remaining tails spread wider. A new wall appeared across the open side, showing the same repaired crack with such care that Nil almost trusted it.

Hina stamped one sandal on the actual platform.

“It can copy that,” she said, angry now. “The real one still hasn't moved.”

The next return action pushed harder against the frame. Nil let the false zero pass and waited for the small engraved mark. They caught 7 instead, the familiar slant their hand knew even when their eyes did not.

The declared return reflected once through the challenge channel. Another tail gauge fell. The pressure stopped. Nil's own stake frame remained above its closing line, although much lower than before.

Tamamo's mouth tightened. Under the image of a serene receiving hall, someone was working very hard to keep control.

“You can make a safe island,” Nil said. “And still keep the only boat.”

“I have kept boats running when the mainland would not send a rope.”

“Then let the keepers help decide where they go.”

The next image arrived with no warning of its meaning. Ofuku's workshop stood at the edge of the room, its new low hook empty. The umbrella on the workbench was only an umbrella. Hina stared at it.

“That's not a forecast,” Aka said sharply.

Tamamo's eyes moved to him.

“A reminder,” she said.

“Then mark it as one.”

For a moment Nil thought Ao would silence his brother. Instead Ao lifted a witness card where everyone could see it.

**IMAGE. NOT EVIDENCE OF AN EVENT.**

The workshop vanished.

Hina was very pale. She took one slow breath, then another. Her hand found the restored patch without looking.

“Don't do that again,” she said.

Tamamo lowered one tail. “I accept the limit.”

It was not an apology large enough to empty the room. It was a condition the witnesses could now enforce.

Nil returned to the wheel. They had caught their breath, not acquired perfect calm. The next stop would still require their hand to wait.

Seven.

Vesper read it, then followed their agreed signal. She stopped 7. Carry made the scoring face 8.

Nil saw a 9 approach under an image of 4. Hina's eye stayed fixed on the actual engraving.

“Nine,” she said.

Nil accepted it.

Seven, eight, nine. The Cascade carried the close through the exposed routes in order. Several tail gauges emptied; their digits dimmed. The room shortened. The extra floor vanished. Three active vessels remained, and their channels were suddenly easy to follow.

The Cascade had held the next court action closed. Nil had a bonus round before the shutters could move again.

Hina looked at the three live routes. One passed beneath the place where the false workshop had stood. Her fear had not left. It was simply no longer the only thing in her face.

“If I keep this patch,” she said to Tamamo, “it has to be because I want it. Not because a contract says this is the last Hina there can be.”

Tamamo said nothing.

“I'm ninety-nine. I want another year. I want lots of them.” Hina's voice shook on the next sentence. “But I'm here now.”

Nil took 9. The actual strike lifted Carry's pin.

Vesper stopped 8. The scoring face advanced once, to 9. She moved aside so Nil could see the last wheel without looking around her shoulder.

Hina raised the umbrella, bringing the old patch between the projection lamp and the final rim. Its crooked edge cut a thin clear shadow across the real engraving.

Nil stopped 9.

Three nines locked in the scoring frame. A Jackpot under Hina's authorized route, not Nil's chosen-seven pattern, not a new birthday.

The last three vessels settled to their closing lines. The projected walls collapsed into ordinary light. Tamamo's actual nine tails remained behind her, no longer carrying an active challenge digit. Her Civic still read 99.

She sat on the edge of the platform before her knees could choose a less dignified place.

Nobody cheered.

The silence held the sound from the care wing: an attendant setting a basin down, a patient asking whether the window could be opened.

Hina looked toward it.

“They still need the renewals,” she said.

Tamamo nodded. “Yes.”

“The local gate tomorrow. With Ofuku. With their keepers.”

“As witnessed.”

Ao disconnected the challenge vessels. Aka checked the unused load returning to its source. Maeve inspected the isolation joint once more before signing the closure. The care-line gauge in the next wing had never entered the wager.

Vesper went to the rack, checked the seal and took back the revolver with its five live rounds. Her hands shook a little while she fastened the holster. Nil was relieved to see it; for a moment they had thought everyone but them could leave a hard thing behind simply by standing up.

Tamamo rose. Ao offered an arm. She accepted it without turning the help into another ceremony.

“I do not know how to promise a future without promising against loss,” she told Hina.

Hina looked at the paper body she had nearly been offered the right to keep forever.

“Then don't promise that part yet.”

Outside, the evening wind reached the court steps. Hina opened her umbrella against it, and the little raised corner of the patch lifted. Maeve saw it. She kept her hands at her sides.

After a while Hina held the shaft toward her.

“You can carry it to the gate,” she said. “Don't fold it.”

Maeve accepted it with both hands.

Nil walked beside Hina down the real stairs. At the old tree, the sign still pointed stubbornly through the trunk. Hina glanced at it and almost laughed, then put a sleeve over her eyes instead.

They waited. She had asked for a future; she did not owe them a happy face before the end of the day.
