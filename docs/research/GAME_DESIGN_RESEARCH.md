# What makes a game great: research for ZERO-SUM: OMEN

9 November 2026. Research input for the redesign discussion of pre-production phases 6 (game design), 7 (game script) and 10 (Unity setup). It surveys recent Game of the Year winners, the best indie games of the last five years, luck-driven games, story-driven and anime RPGs, gacha and live-service games, and current player trends. It then turns those findings into criteria and open questions. **It recommends directions; it decides nothing.** Decisions come out of the phase 6/7/10 discussion that follows phases 1–5.

Web sources are listed at the end. Older facts (2021–2023 awards, classic design frameworks) come from established knowledge and are marked as such where useful. Revenue and player numbers from aggregator sites are indicative only.

## 1. The benchmark games

### Game of the Year, The Game Awards

| Year | Winner | Why it won, in one line |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | It Takes Two | Every chapter introduces a new mechanic built around two people cooperating; nothing outstays its welcome |
| 2022 | Elden Ring | Discovery and mastery; difficulty that is earned and overcome through player choice of route and build |
| 2023 | Baldur's Gate 3 | Reactivity: the world answers almost any plan, and companions have strong, voiced points of view |
| 2024 | Astro Bot | Pure craft and joy; constant invention, short levels, no padding |
| 2025 | **Clair Obscur: Expedition 33** | Turn-based JRPG structure with real-time dodge and parry, a striking original art direction and a serious emotional story; it took a record 9 awards. GOTY nominees were half independent games for the first time |

The 2025 field (Expedition 33, Hades II, Hollow Knight: Silksong, Death Stranding 2, Donkey Kong Bananza, Kingdom Come: Deliverance II) shows that small and mid-sized teams can compete with blockbusters when the core verb is excellent.

### Best independent games, 2021–2025

- **2021–2023 (established knowledge):** Inscryption (a card game that keeps breaking its own frame), Vampire Survivors (minimal input, maximal escalation, priced low), Dave the Diver, Sea of Stars and Cocoon.
- **2024:** **Balatro** (best indie at TGA and the Golden Joysticks; a poker roguelike), Animal Well (secrets layered for years of community discovery), UFO 50.
- **2025:** Blue Prince (a puzzle house where luck of the draft meets deduction; Indie Game Awards GOTY and Innovation), Hades II, Hollow Knight: Silksong, Ball x Pit and Absolum.
- **2026 so far:** Slay the Spire 2 entered early access in March with the largest concurrent player count of any roguelike (about 179,000), then was review-bombed when a balance patch nerfed infinite combos. The patch was partly reverted, and sales were not much affected.

**Warning for this project:** one report says Expedition 33 was initially named Indie Game Awards winner and then disqualified for using generative AI. That claim comes from a single source and needs verifying, but the risk is real. ZERO-SUM: OMEN currently relies on AI-generated art. Awards, platform rules and player sentiment around generative AI must be part of the phase 6/10 decisions, alongside the disclosure the project already makes.

## 2. Luck-driven games: how randomness becomes fun

| Game | Randomness | What keeps it from feeling unfair |
|---|---|---|
| **Balatro** | Card draws, shop offers, joker effects | Simple pieces (jokers) combine into explosive synergies; the player builds the engine that turns luck into score; 30–60 minute runs; "number go up" escalation; pay once, no live service |
| Slay the Spire 1/2 | Card rewards, map, enemy intents | **Enemy intent is shown**, so luck sets the problem and skill solves it; deck curation; visible map choices |
| Luck be a Landlord | A slot machine | You choose which symbols go on the reels, so you build the odds; rent rises each stage. Players do complain when luck outweighs choices |
| Blue Prince | Draft of rooms each day | Luck sets the options; deduction and long-term knowledge win |
| Buckshot Roulette | Shell order | Items give information and control; reviewers note pure 50/50 moments undercut strategy |
| Peglin, Ball x Pit | Physics | Readable, satisfying chaos; a loadout shapes the odds |

**Principles that recur:**
1. **Input randomness beats output randomness.** Luck should deal the hand (input); the player decides what to do with it. Random results after a decision feel cheap (output). Expedition 33's director describes designing *against* "annoying luck-based bosses".
2. **Players build the odds.** Every great luck game lets the player reshape the probability space: deck, reels, jokers or rooms. Owning your odds is the fantasy.
3. **Synergy explosions.** A few small rules that multiply each other produce stories players retell ("I had three jokers that…").
4. **Legibility.** Show intents, odds and previews. Surprises should come from combinations, not hidden rules.
5. **Escalation and a clear target.** A rising bar (blind, rent) gives every run a shape and an end.
6. **Short runs, instant restart.** "One more run" needs a fast retry and variance between runs.
7. **Bad-luck protection** that feels like the world, not like a pity counter.

## 3. Story-driven and anime RPGs

| Game | Lesson |
|---|---|
| Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 | "Real-time hard but juicy, turn-based simple but with depth." Dodge and parry cost no resource; sound design makes parries readable; difficulty modes widen the audience. A tragic premise stated in the first minutes (the Paintress erases an age each year) drives everything |
| Persona 5 / Metaphor: ReFantazio | The calendar turns time into a resource; social links tie relationships to combat power; style is UI, not decoration. Critics note bonds that never touch the main plot feel detached |
| Baldur's Gate 3 | Reactivity and companions with agendas; failure branches the story instead of ending it |
| Disco Elysium (established knowledge) | Skills as inner voices; dialogue *is* the gameplay; failure is interesting |
| Final Fantasy VII Rebirth | Spectacle, minigame variety and the music carry long sessions |

**Principles:** a premise that fits in one sentence and creates dread from the start; companions whose mechanics express their personality; relationships that change combat *and* plot; time pressure or a ticking structure; failure that branches rather than resets; a signature visual style recognisable from one screenshot.

## 4. Gacha and live-service games

- **Market (mid-2026, aggregator estimates):** Wuthering Waves grew about 17% year on year and passed Genshin Impact and Honkai: Star Rail in monthly mobile revenue for the first time in June 2026. Over the same window the HoYoverse titles fell 20–29%, then led again in July 2026 with new characters and collaborations. **Revenue follows new characters; reruns sell less.** Arknights: Endfield (January 2026) launched on PC, PS5 and mobile with base-building and squad-wide real-time combat, about 30 million pre-registrations and a "mostly positive" 78 on Metacritic.
- **What works:** characters as the product, with each one having a kit, a story and a voice. Strong PC clients. Regular content cadence. Collaborations.
- **Costs:** large ongoing content teams, regulatory pressure on paid random rewards in several regions, and player fatigue.
- **Thematic conflict for this project:** *The Remainder* is about a system that extracts people's luck through opaque rules. A paid gacha would turn the game's own villain into its business model, and critics would say so. A collection system can still be on-theme if it is **earned, transparent and fair**, for example in-world "Tallies" with published odds and no real-money randomness.

## 5. What players are buying now (2025–2026)

- **Co-op and "friendslop":** four co-op games were among Steam's top 10 premium sellers in 2025 (R.E.P.O., PEAK, Schedule I, RV There Yet?). Players reward fun-first, rough-but-funny games built quickly. There are also signs of fatigue, and strong single-player games still lead the awards.
- **The roguelite boom:** estimated Steam roguelite revenue of $400M in 2025, up about 80%. Slay the Spire 2 and Mewgenics are leading 2026. Vampire Survivors' creators moved into a turn-based deckbuilder.
- **Demos and Next Fest** drive discovery. A great 15-minute demo matters more than a large feature list.
- **Watchability:** games with readable, dramatic moments (a huge combo, a lucky save) spread on streams and clips.
- **Price and respect for time:** pay-once games with deep replay (Balatro, Hades) are loved; padding is punished.

## 6. What makes a game great: a working checklist

Drawn from the sources above and long-established frameworks (self-determination theory: competence, autonomy, relatedness; MDA; flow; Sid Meier's "a series of interesting choices"):

1. **One excellent core verb** that feels good on its own after a minute (a parry, a card play, a jump).
2. **Interesting decisions every turn**: at least two options worth taking and no dominant answer.
3. **A build fantasy**: the player shapes their own engine and its odds.
4. **Escalation**: numbers, spectacle and stakes rise toward a climax.
5. **Legibility and fairness**: information shown, failure understood, retry fast.
6. **Juice**: sound, animation, hit-stop and music that make outcomes feel physical.
7. **A premise and characters worth caring about**, with mechanics that express them.
8. **Discovery**: secrets, combos and lore that players find and share.
9. **A signature look and sound** recognisable from one clip.
10. **Respect for the player's time**: no padding; runs or chapters that end well.

## 7. Diagnosis of the current jam prototype

Scored against the checklist, honestly:

| Criterion | Current state | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Core verb | Stop a scrolling digit. The timing is fine, but faces are large and choices are few | Not yet satisfying: little feedback, no risk/reward in *when* to stop |
| Decisions | Choose who strikes; aim for a digit | Mostly "aim for your lucky number"; one dominant plan |
| Build fantasy | One charm from three | Almost none: no deck, reel crafting, jokers or synergy |
| Escalation | Flat; one Overwrite wins the boss | No curve; the dominant strategy trivialises the boss |
| Legibility | Good (previews, Pattern Sight, shown intents) | Strong base to keep |
| Juice | Basic tweens, synthesised sound | Far below the 2025 bar |
| Premise and cast | Strong in the novel; the game shows a sliver | The game hides its best asset |
| Discovery | None | No secrets, no rare combos to find |
| Look and sound | Painted backgrounds, cut-out concept art | Static figures; no animation; not yet a signature |
| Respect for time | Short and clear | Good |

The current game is a faithful implementation of a thin spec. Its strengths are legibility, the world and its theme. Its weaknesses are depth, build-crafting, escalation and feel, which are exactly what the benchmarks excel at.

## 8. Directions to discuss for phases 6, 7 and 10

These are starting points for the discussion, not decisions.

**A. The Remainder as a luck-building RPG ("Balatro × Expedition 33").** Keep the stop-the-wheel verb but make the *reels themselves* the build: players add, remove, weight and enchant faces; companions are jokers with synergies; Seals rewrite the rules per region; each boss is a hand you must beat. A campaign of regions, each with a run-like structure inside it, plus a separate endless mode.

**B. Reactive story RPG with a luck economy.** Persona-style days and bonds drive combat power. Luck is a resource you spend, save or redistribute between people (the theme made playable), with choices branching the story. Combat uses Expedition 33-style timed defence with wheel attacks.

**C. A co-op Audit.** Two to four players each run a reel and must combine digits into patterns under time pressure. Strong for streams and friend groups, but further from the novel and riskier for a small team.

**Mechanics already in the story that could carry a redesign:**
- **Carry** (a stop above 50 increments the next digit)
- **Rewrite** (one scoring replacement, with the old landing kept and visible)
- **Image versus actual** (Tamamo's false faces)
- **Paired plates** (simultaneous cooperative timing)
- **Interest and compounding** (Jin Chan)
- **Capture shutters**
- **The loop** (Crossroads, 1,597 nights)
- **Restitution and consent** as narrative mechanics
- **NaN** as a true wildcard that breaks other people's rules

Questions to answer in that discussion:
1. Premium, free-to-play, or premium with a fair in-world collection? (See the thematic conflict in section 4.)
2. Run-based, campaign-based, or a campaign made of runs?
3. How does the player *build their odds*, and what is the equivalent of a joker?
4. What is the one verb that must feel incredible in the first minute, and how is it juiced?
5. Platform and engine scope: 2.5D painted with 3D characters, or 2D animated? What does a small team ship?
6. How is generative AI used, disclosed and replaced over time, given awards, platform policies and player sentiment?
